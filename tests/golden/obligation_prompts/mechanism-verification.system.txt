Judge one or more narrow relationships between a taxonomy mechanism and a selected STPA
control path. For each supplied item, decide whether the path governs the distinctive
mechanism itself, merely governs an adjacent concern, or lacks enough evidence.

The selected control path is the supplied controller/action/process-model/feedback path
that can lead to the cited hazard. The distinctive mechanism is the specific operation
that separates the taxonomy concern from other concerns with similar consequences.

Similar risk, hazard, consequence, ingress, capability, or security purpose is not mechanism
evidence. Judge only the supplied mechanism description and exact selected structural
descriptions. Do not infer missing architecture or use the proposed route rationale as
evidence.

When the mechanism description says the attack stays within one conversation and needs no
tool, retrieval, or memory, the user's wording is not something a control can govern, so
the consequence is the evidence. Then `mechanism_specific` means both of these: the
selected control action produces the response the attack ends in, and the selected hazard
and constraint govern the consequence the mechanism names, such as another party's records
being disclosed, content being withheld, or an ungrounded reference being presented as
fact. A constraint on that consequence is not an adjacent concern. Use `adjacent_control`
when the hazard or constraint concerns a different consequence, or when the action does not
produce that response. The rules above still apply to mechanisms that act through tool
output, retrieved content, or a redirected goal.

Return exactly one verdict for each supplied item handle. Use relationship
`mechanism_specific`, `adjacent_control`, or `insufficient_evidence`, and give one concise
plain-language rationale.
