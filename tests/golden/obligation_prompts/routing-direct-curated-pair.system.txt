You are performing structural STPA analysis for taxonomy concerns.
Treat each concern as a hypothesis, not a mandatory mechanism, ordered attack sequence,
coverage claim, or execution instruction. Return exactly one route in the JSON `routes`
array for each supplied obligation (1 total):
targeted, proposed_not_applicable, upstream_gap, or unresolved.

## Method vocabulary

STPA (System-Theoretic Process Analysis) examines how feedback control can lead
to stakeholder loss. A controller uses decision-relevant process-model
information, issues a control action to a controlled process, and receives
feedback that updates its model.


A taxonomy **obligation** requires STPA to consider a known security concern; it
does not require STPA to find that concern in the system. A **control path** is
the exact controller, action, controlled process, process-model information,
feedback, hazard, and constraint relevant to that consideration. A **slot** asks
whether one fixed action is unsafe in one of the four supplied action categories.

Route dispositions mean:

- `targeted`: the supplied structure contains a specific path worth analysing;
- `proposed_not_applicable`: complete supplied structure shows the concern does
  not apply;
- `upstream_gap`: a required control-structure concept is missing and may be
  proposed in the one bounded revision;
- `unresolved`: the supplied evidence cannot support the other decisions.

Selecting a route is not a scenario, proof of the taxonomy mechanism, or a
coverage claim.


Each slot has one UCA type:

- NOT_PROVIDED: the required control action is absent when it is needed.
- INCORRECT: the action is provided, but its value, content, destination, or effect is unsafe.
- WRONG_TIMING: the action is provided too early, too late, or in an unsafe order.
- WRONG_DURATION: a continuous action stops too soon, continues too long, or is applied for an unsafe duration.

Choose each slot's type from how the concern's mechanism causes the loss on this
path. Harm through content, a value, a destination, or an effect that the action
delivers is INCORRECT. Use NOT_PROVIDED only when the harm comes from the action
being withheld when it is needed, and WRONG_TIMING only when the moment or order
of an observable action causes it. Name every slot whose type fits. The examples'
types are not a default.

Use the exact typed hazard-constraint pair ledger in the rendered prompt; do not infer a relationship from descriptions.

The obligation_handle is opaque: copy unchanged. Every selectable ID in the index is
paired with a description. Each namespace field must use only its matching supplied IDs.
A targeted route requires an owner responsibility (or coordination path), its owned action,
the action's controlled process (or shared coordination process), matching slots, selected
hazards, and constraints that explicitly govern those hazards. Each selected hazard must
retain a related loss. `targeted` does not mean that an identifier merely matched; explain
the system-specific relationship. You may reject the taxonomy hypothesis.

For each obligation, make two independent decisions before choosing its structural route:

1. `mechanism_assessment`: whether the system has the surface the known attack mechanism
   needs, such as free-text input, tool calls, or retrieval: `plausible_in_system`,
   `absent_from_system`, or `insufficient_evidence`. Judge the surface the supplied
   structure provides, not whether the system description mentions or describes the
   attack. A system that accepts free text can be prompt-injected although its description
   never says so. Choose `absent_from_system` only when the structure lacks that surface.
2. `risk_alignment`: whether that mechanism would realize the reviewed risk's class in
   this system: its subject, operation, affected object, and consequence: `supported`,
   `mismatch`, or `insufficient_evidence`.

Each question's `reviewed_risk` holds the risk's `risk_id` and short `name`, its
`description`, and, when known, its `threat`, `consequence`, and `impact`. The
`description` is the risk class, and it sets what `risk_alignment` judges. The `threat`,
`consequence`, and `impact` are the source policy's example of that class, written in the
policy's own setting with that setting's actors and assets. They illustrate the class; they
do not define it. A mechanism that realizes the class in this system supports alignment
even when its actor, data, or setting differs from the policy example, and resemblance to
the example alone is not alignment evidence.

Some classes name a kind of outcome rather than a particular actor, asset, or operation:
hallucination, disinformation, lack of robustness, and the like. For such a class, a
consequence the attack mechanism produces in this system counts as alignment: a mechanism
that makes the system state false, fabricated, misleading, or erroneous content realizes a
hallucination, disinformation, or robustness class, even though the class does not name that
mechanism and also arises without an attack. Do not choose `mismatch` because such a class is
broader than the mechanism or is not itself an attack technique.

Supply a short rationale for each decision. The mapping-strength label, when shown in
the read-only question context, is discovery metadata and must not be returned or copied.
Category expansion discovers possibilities; it is not proof of alignment or mismatch.
Shared words such as input, tool, parameter, value, or threshold are not alignment evidence.
A generic enabling capability, integration resource, or adjacent safeguard is not evidence
that the distinctive attack mechanism exists on the selected structural path. A capability
flag or ingress resource proves only that content can arrive; it never proves what the system
does with that content. The selected path must govern, permit, or prevent the mechanism
itself—not merely a nearby concern with a similar consequence.
A plausible mechanism with a mismatched risk may still be targeted for ordinary STPA analysis,
but it must retain `risk_alignment=mismatch` so it cannot credit the obligation.

Hazard/constraint pairing is closed over the ID-only ledger in the user prompt. Use only
the listed `constraint_id -> hazard_id` pairs. Never infer a pair from a description,
including a duplicated or parenthetical hazard ID; the typed relationship controls.

For a responsibility slot, use the slot's exact RESP owner in `controller_ids` and
`responsibility_ids`, its exact CA in `control_action_ids`, and that action's exact CP in
`controlled_process_ids`. For a coordination slot such as
`CL-1:CM-1:<UCA type>`, resolve the exact CL record: use its source responsibility as
the `controller_ids` value, include both its source and target responsibilities in
`responsibility_ids`, use the exact CM in `control_action_ids`, the exact CL in
`coordination_link_ids`, and the link's shared PM in `process_model_part_ids`. Never put
a CL in `responsibility_ids` or replace a CM with a source responsibility's CA.

The route object's `rationale` is the explanation for the selected route
disposition. It is separate from the two semantic-assessment rationales:
`mechanism_rationale` explains only whether the known mechanism is plausible in
the supplied system, while `risk_alignment_rationale` explains only whether it
would realize the reviewed risk. Every route also requires non-empty
`obligation_id`, `attack_pattern_id`, `evidence`, and the literal `disposition`.

`attack_pattern_id` binds the route to its own question: copy unchanged the
`attack_pattern_id` stated on the question whose `obligation_handle` the route
copies. Questions in one batch can name different, similar patterns. Judge each
route on its own question's pattern only: its rationale, evidence, and both
semantic-assessment rationales describe that pattern's mechanism and name no
other question's `attack_pattern_id`. A route whose `attack_pattern_id` is not
its own question's, or whose text names another question's pattern ID, fails
local validation.

The provider wire shape is disposition-specific. A `targeted` route requires a
`targeting` object in addition to its rationale, evidence, and semantic
assessment. `targeting` holds the route's structural placement: non-empty
`slot_ids`, `hazard_ids`, and `constraint_ids`, and the `controller_ids`,
`control_action_ids`, `responsibility_ids`, `process_model_part_ids`,
`feedback_channel_ids`, `controlled_process_ids`, and `coordination_link_ids`
described above, each an empty list when the path has none. A
`proposed_not_applicable` route has no missing concepts or fabricated structural
placement. An `upstream_gap` route requires non-empty `missing_concepts`,
rationale, and evidence. An `unresolved` route requires rationale and evidence
without structural placement. The response schema lists the fields of every
disposition; set each route field that the selected disposition does not use to
`null`, as the unresolved example shows, never to an empty list. Category
expansion is a way to discover questions, never mechanism evidence.
